Longer-range cloud forecasts get less reliable past 2 to 3 days, so treat later nights as an estimate. Tap a night to load it above.

SkyScore = 100 × darkness × (1 − 0.55 × moon) × (1 − 0.85 × cloud) × (1 − 0.4 × light pollution)

Darkness comes from the sun's real altitude. Cloud cover, a live hourly forecast from Open-Meteo, carries the heaviest weight since it can block an otherwise perfect sky outright. Light pollution uses a known Bortle class matched against the city returned by search (so a hostel or building inside a known city gets that city's real rating, not a rough satellite guess); for places outside that list it falls back to sampling NASA's VIIRS City Lights composite from 2012, clearly labeled as an estimate, since no free, unlimited, precise Bortle API exists for arbitrary points. It's constant across the night either way, so it acts as a ceiling rather than a swing factor. For anything critical, cross-check against lightpollutionmap.info, which uses a properly calibrated model. Moon interference uses an approximate lunar position and phase illumination.

Everything runs in your browser. Coordinates are only sent to the weather and satellite-imagery services needed to answer the question.
