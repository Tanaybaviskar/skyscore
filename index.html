SkyScore = 100 × darkness × (1 − 0.55 × moon) × (1 − 0.85 × cloud) × (1 − 0.4 × light pollution)

Darkness comes from the sun's real altitude (0 at civil twilight, 1 once the sun is 18° below the horizon). Moon interference uses an approximate lunar position and phase illumination. Cloud cover is a live hourly forecast from Open-Meteo and carries the heaviest weight, since it can block an otherwise perfect sky outright. Light pollution is sampled directly from NASA's VIIRS City Lights satellite composite (via NASA GIBS) at your exact coordinates and converted to an approximate Bortle class — it doesn't change hour to hour, so it acts as a constant ceiling on how dark your sky can ever get. Everything runs in your browser; your coordinates are only sent to the weather and satellite-imagery services needed to answer the question.
